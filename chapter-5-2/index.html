Why this session

The AIC-DC licence is adopted for a selected deployment, not for the entire organization.

  • A deployment may be a small part of what we do, or all of it.
  • Identifying the deployment is the first step of licence adoption, and every later step rests on it.
  • We may license many deployments. We don't have to bring them all into the framework at once.

Two terms

A deployment and its deliverable

  • A deployment is a working arrangement of processes that puts AI capabilities to work to produce one deliverable.
  • The deliverable is what we declare the deployment to produce: a product, a service, an offer, or an AI capability itself.
  • The deliverable settles which deployment it is, and where it begins and ends among everything we do with AI.

What counts as AI here

The framework understands AI capability widely.

  • An AI capability is what an AI system can do, as our processes put it to use.
  • A model we run ourselves counts, and so does a feature inside a product we buy.
  • So does any other automated computation that does such work at machine scale, whether or not anyone calls it artificial intelligence.
  • The capability may be our own, come from elsewhere in our organization, or be supplied by another party.

A real choice

A deliverable can often be identified in more than one honest way.

  • Picture a firm that grows and delivers fresh flowers through a largely automated operation.
  • The firm may select the flowers as its deliverable. The deployment then takes in the whole production of the flowers, with the labour and the materials involved, and the deployment's revenue is the flower sales.
  • Or it may select the automated growing-and-dispatch service as its deliverable. The deployment is then drawn more tightly around that service, and the deployment's revenue is a share of the final price.
  • Both choices are honest. The question for us is which deliverable we want the world to know as produced under the AIC-DC licence.

A platform and its products

The same choice comes up with a platform and its products.

  • The platform is the deliverable, and the products draw on it.
  • A product is the deliverable, and takes its share of the platform into its scope.
  • Or both are declared, each with a deployment of its own.

One deliverable, or several?

A family of offerings can be one deliverable.

  • The family holds together while its offerings share one cycle of value and come out of the same processes, with the same kind of inputs and outputs.
  • Copies of one service run for different clients are one deployment, so long as each is the same service, sold on the same terms.
  • Where the processes or the economics diverge too far, the production of each is a deployment of its own.

One deployer

Each deployment has one deployer.

  • The deployer is the legal actor that runs the deployment and will issue the licence.
  • Where several legal actors run the operation together, one of them has to be the deployer.
  • The deployer will coordinate the adoption and management of the licence, and answer for it across the whole deployment, including the parts the others run.

Question 4 of 5

Candidate deliverable

Is the product or service a single offering, or a family of offerings?

A single offering

One deliverable and one deployment. We go on to the next question.

A family that holds together

The offerings share one cycle of value and one set of processes, with the same kind of inputs and outputs. The family is one deliverable, and one deployment produces it.

A family that diverges

The processes or the economics differ too far. We treat the offerings as separate deployments, and each becomes a candidate of its own.

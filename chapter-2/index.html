The architecture of the framework

A licence in one small figure

This small sign states what one deployment of AI commits to, and what it requires of others.

  • Six letters, three columns of circles, and a code.
  • Every AIC-DC licence is encoded in a sign like this one.
  • By the end of this presentation we will be able to read from this sign what this particular deployment's relationship with the common good is.

What a licence is

A licence is a permission.

  • Whoever holds something exclusively, for instance land or an invention, can let others do with it, or within it, what they otherwise could not.
  • The permission can be given on conditions.
  • An AIC-DC licence starts from an organization's control over its own deployment of AI.
  • The licence lets in parties whose interests would otherwise stay outside that deployment, and gives them rights in it.

What the licence attaches to

A deployment and its deliverable

  • A deployment is a working arrangement of processes that puts AI capabilities to work to produce one deliverable.
  • The deliverable is what we declare the deployment to produce: a product, a service, an offer, or an AI capability itself.
  • The deliverable draws the boundary of the deployment.
  • The deliverable need not contain any AI.

The deployer and the deployment

The licence applies to the deployment. The organization that runs it is the deployer.

  • A deployment can be a single service, or the whole operation of a firm that runs on AI.
  • Even then the firm and the deployment stay distinct: everything the licence does, including ending a deployment, applies to the deployment.
  • A deployer may run many deployments.
  • A deployment has one deployer: where several organizations carry it out together, the deployer is the one that issues the licence.

Deployments in a chain

One deployment's deliverable can be the next one's AI capability.

  • An upstream supplier is a deployment whose deliverable we use as an AI capability.
  • A downstream operator is a deployment that uses our deliverable as an AI capability, building it into a product or service of its own.
  • For example, a firm that serves a foundation model is the upstream supplier of a business that builds a service on that model.
  • A deployment may have many of each.

Commitment and requirement

What we hold ourselves, and what we ask of our neighbours

  • A commitment is a profile we adopt for our own deployment.
  • A requirement makes a profile a condition of our dealings with our suppliers or with the operators we supply.
  • Upstream requirement: we draw on no supplier's AI capability unless that supplier holds the profile as its own commitment.
  • Downstream requirement: an operator may build on our deliverable only if it has adopted the profile for its own operation.

A requirement is a term of our agreement with the other party, so it reaches only those who choose to deal with us.

Two examples

A profile can bind more than one position.

  • We hold the profile ourselves, and require it of our suppliers and of the operators we supply.
  • We hold the profile ourselves, and require it of the operators we supply.

For each profile of the AIC-DC licence we declare which positions it binds. This is the profile's propagation pattern.

The licence profiles

Six profiles, each a standardized grant of rights

  • A licence profile grants one class of outside interest rights in the deployment, and binds us to the obligations needed to honour those rights.
  • The parties who hold those rights are the evoked parties, and they can enforce them against us.
  • The filled-in figure is called the deployment matrix. Each licence profile has a row in it.
  • The three columns are the three positions: upstream, core, and downstream.

Profile 1 of 6 · AIC-DC-R

Reciprocity

We recognize and reward the completed work that went into building and running the deployment, whether or not it was paid for.

  • Anyone who did such work may claim recognition, and at least 5 per cent of the deployment's revenue goes to a remuneration pool.
  • Example: filled at the core. This deployment holds Reciprocity itself, and the code gains an R.

Profile 2 of 6 · AIC-DC-S

Sustainability

The deployment's effect on each of the nine planetary boundaries is neutral at minimum.

  • Each burden is answered by a recovery on the same boundary, in the environment that bore it. No credit may be bought.
  • Example: filled upstream only. This deployment requires Sustainability of its suppliers and does not hold it itself, so the code gains no letter.

Profile 3 of 6 · AIC-DC-O

Openness

The deployment's full operational logic is legible and available for scrutiny.

  • A maintained public record gives access to the code we write and the weights we train or change, describes the data sources, and records the evaluations and incidents of the AI capabilities. Anyone may inspect the record and put questions to it.
  • Example: three open circles. Openness is not in play.

Profile 4 of 6 · AIC-DC-G

Governance

The people the deployment materially affects hold binding power in governing its systemic effects, and the power to discontinue it.

  • A governance body of the affected groups must consent to any weakening of the commitments in the AIC-DC licence, and may itself strengthen them, for instance by adding a profile.
  • Example: filled at the core, and the code now reads AIC-DC-RG.

Profile 5 of 6 · AIC-DC-A

Access

Every natural person may use the deliverable free of charge, at its full level.

  • People get the same deliverable that paying customers get, at the best level on offer. We may limit only how much each person uses, and never to less than ordinary personal use needs.
  • Example: filled in all three columns. This deployment holds Access and requires it of its whole chain, and the code now reads AIC-DC-RGA.

Profile 6 of 6 · AIC-DC-V

Value

At least 5 per cent of the deployment's revenue funds a basic income: a regular cash payment, with no conditions, to every resident of an area we choose.

  • Until global poverty is ended, the area must be one where the rate of extreme poverty is at least twice the world's rate. An independent non-profit or public body runs the scheme and makes the payments.
  • Example: three open circles. Value is not in play.

The choice is ours

Which profiles a licence carries is our choice, made for each deployment.

  • A licence carries at least one profile. One profile is enough, and we may add others later.
  • Not every deployment can hold every profile. A deployment that earns no revenue, for instance, has nothing on which Reciprocity or Value could be reckoned.

Reading the matrix

Now we can read it.

  • Reciprocity and Governance: held by this deployment.
  • Sustainability: required of the upstream suppliers.
  • Access: held, and required of the whole chain.
  • The deployment code names only what the deployment itself holds: AIC-DC-RGA.

The licence text

The text of the licence is fixed, and published in the Standard.

  • The Universal Terms apply to every AIC-DC licence.
  • A Module for each profile holds that profile's terms.
  • Each profile has a few parameters, which we set to our circumstances within the range the profile permits.
  • Beyond its parameters the text cannot be altered, so a profile grants the same rights wherever it is adopted.

The public record

The declaration and its records stay public, on our own domain.

  • We make the licence by signing a deployment declaration and publishing it at a web address we control. No central register has to admit it.
  • The record comes in two forms with the same content: one for human readers, opening with the matrix, and one machine-readable, at a fixed address.
  • Any reader or registry can find the records and compare one deployment with another.
  • The record is cumulative. A change is made by re-issuing the declaration, and what was published before stays published.
